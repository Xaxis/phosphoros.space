# About

## What Phosphoros is

Phosphoros is a speculative but honest engineering plan. It asks what it would take to make Venus
Earth-like in ten years, from 2027 to 2036, and answers with a complete, internally consistent design:
twelve inventions, a decade of dates, and an energy budget that shows where it closes and where it
does not. It is a design review of an
impossible-sounding idea, carried out in public.

Three rules make it honest rather than merely bold:

- **Every number is reproducible.** Each one comes from [`data/`](../data/) through the model in
  [`calc/`](../calc/), and the [ledger](numbers.md) shows its formula, its inputs and the line that computes
  it. Change an assumption and every page follows.
- **Every real-world claim cites a source**, listed in [Sources](sources.md).
- **Every invention says it is one.** Where the plan assumes engineering that does not exist, the page says
  so, and the data marks the number as invented.

Physics is the one thing it never assumes away. Every invention obeys conservation of energy and the second
law of thermodynamics, and the model checks the plan against both on every run.

## What it is not

It is not a proposal to start tomorrow, and not a prediction that anyone will. Most of its twelve
inventions are far beyond what exists, and several may prove impossible: the [Research Agenda](research.md)
says which experiments would show that. It is not an argument that Venus should be changed. If the clouds
hold life, the plan stops, and even if they do not, the decision belongs to everyone, not to a program
([Risks and Governance](risks.md)).

What it is useful for is the map. It shows which inventions a fast terraforming would need, how good each
would have to be, and which laws no amount of invention gets around. That is worth knowing whether or not
anyone ever builds it.

## The name

Phosphoros is Greek for "light-bringer", the ancient name for Venus as the morning star. Its evening name was
Hesperos, which survives here in the [Hesperus Veil](technologies/veil.md). The name has a second meaning
for this plan: Phosphoros cools Venus by beaming its heat away into space as light.

## Changelog

The project began as **Project Hesperus**, a conventional analysis that concluded an Earth-like Venus in ten
years was impossible without imported energy, and proposed a decade of groundwork for a transformation
lasting centuries. Its second version, **Project Hesperus Ten**, asked what would have to be invented to
finish in ten years instead, and was renamed Phosphoros. The third version, published on 2026-11-20, rebuilt
every number from first principles in code. That reproduced almost all of version 2, and found the errors
listed below.

Version 3 changed how the plan is made, not only its numbers:

- Every number is computed by the model in [`calc/`](../calc/) from [`data/`](../data/), and links to its
  formula, its inputs and the line that defines it.
- A day-by-day simulation of Venus's air drives the energy budget and the finish date, instead of averages.
- Physics checks run on every change: energy, the second law, the forces on everything that hovers or
  hangs, machine capacities and the order of events. Failing checks are shown, not hidden.
- Every number in the version 2 brief is [checked against the model](#version-2s-numbers-checked).

Version 4, published on 2026-11-23, closed the five open problems version 3 ended with by changing the
design, never the physics:

- The Braking Ring is retired. Nothing could hold it against the catch, lower the ice or carry its receivers,
  so the Bricks fall straight into Venus's polar air, and their fall is heat the pumps must lift. That heat
  sets how much water Venus can be given: seas in its lowlands, not Earth's oceans.
- The Lattice's light pumps the Exhaust's tiles directly, as laser cooling does, so only the Railheads, the
  Forges and the fans run on receivers. The tiles shine over their whole sky, and every pump is charged at the
  temperature their light counts as. Only the sunward side, which the Lattice can reach, is pumped.
- The launches are booked on their real dates, from the measured orbits of Ceres and Venus.
- The Forges wait for the seas they float on.

The release notes for each version are in [CHANGELOG.md](../CHANGELOG.md). Every change to a published
number is listed under [Corrections](#corrections), with its reason and the numbers before and after.

### Version 3: Phosphoros, every number reproducible

Every number in the plan now comes from data/ through the model in calc/. Rebuilding the numbers from first principles reproduced almost all of version 2 and found the corrections below.

- **Correction (v3): The Veil goes back to L1, as a light-bending film.** A statite hovers only where light pushes it away from what pulls it. Sunlight travels from the Sun toward Venus, so it pushes a sunward shade toward Venus, the same way Venus's gravity pulls it: the version 2 Veil would fall onto Venus in about a day. The only place a shade can hold station between the Sun and Venus is at or just sunward of the L1 point, 1.01 million km out, where the Sun's pull balances Venus's. Version 3 puts it there and makes it a clear film that bends sunlight a few degrees so it misses Venus, rather than a mirror, so light pressure barely moves it (as in Angel's 2006 design for an Earth sunshade). The cost is area: about 3.6 times version 2's, 5.4 × 10¹¹ kg instead of 1.5 × 10¹¹ kg. The Foundry can afford that by March 2032, so the Veil closes in June 2032 instead of December 2031, still six months before active cooling. The Aegis coil moves with it, to L1, where NASA's Mars shield study put its own. ([issue #1](https://github.com/Xaxis/phosphoros.space/issues/1))
- **Correction (v3): The Exhaust is sized for the peak the simulation needs.** Version 2 sized the Photonic Exhaust from the campaign's average power. The new day-by-day simulation shows the timing matters: until the Ring takes over the Railheads in August 2034 most of the Lattice's power goes to Ceres, and after that power is plentiful but the emitters cannot shine it. The Ring's rotor has 3.0 × 10¹³ N of lift to spare, 150 times too little. Options under study: drop the Bricks from rest at the Ring and pump out the extra 2.5 × 10²⁶ J of heat; lower over a longer time; or a far heavier, faster rotor. The energy budget never counted the lowering's recovered energy, so it is unchanged. Version 4 retires the Ring: the Bricks fall straight into the air. ([issue #2](https://github.com/Xaxis/phosphoros.space/issues/2))

Every Brick arrives from the same direction, and the two streams graze the Ring at mirror-image points, each about 22° forward of the line across Venus. Their sideways pushes cancel and their forward pushes add: 6.0 × 10¹⁶ N along Venus's orbital motion, 2,000 times the rotor's spare lift. An orbital ring has no stiff way to hand that force to the planet. The catch would recover the energy the budget counts; what holds the Ring in place while it does is unsolved. Version 4 retires the Ring. ([issue #3](https://github.com/Xaxis/phosphoros.space/issues/3))

The same dipole from a wider loop needs far less current, and the pull falls with the cube of the radius. Either the tiles spread their light over far more colours and directions, or the pumps need many times the power. The budget's pump work is a floor until this is solved. With the measured aerogel the caps leak 0.16 W/m², 24 TW in all, still small beside the floor heat, and take 2.5 × 10¹⁷ kg of graphite. That curve gave 74 Pa at the Frost Towers' 133 K, and 4.9 bar at the triple point against the measured 5.2 bar, so the simulated pressure stepped down as the air crossed it. Span and Wagner's sublimation equation meets the vapour curve at their triple point, 216.6 K and 5.2 bar, which the model now uses as well, and gives 54 Pa at 133 K. The Frost Towers leave 2.8 × 10¹⁵ kg airborne, and the finished air holds 230 ppm of CO₂. ([issue #10](https://github.com/Xaxis/phosphoros.space/issues/10))
- **Correction (v3): The seas freeze at the triple point, and the simulation's heat books balance.** The simulation released all of the CO₂'s latent heat evenly between its critical point and its triple point, although 5.7% of the CO₂ is still vapour at the triple point and liquid CO₂ cannot freeze above it. Now the CO₂ gives up its heat of condensation as it rains out along the vapour curve; at the triple point the seas give up their heat of fusion, 210 kJ/kg, while the air holds at 216.6 K; and the CO₂ still in the air gives up the whole deposition heat as it freezes out below. - **Correction (v3): The Ceres factories are seeded with the Foundry and must double faster.** Version 2 sent the first Ceres factories from Earth but never sized them. ([issue #7](https://github.com/Xaxis/phosphoros.space/issues/7))
- **Correction (v3): The Foundry's target is what it builds next.** The Foundry's target mass was typed in as 6.0 × 10¹³ kg, about the Lattice and the Ring before the Lattice grew. It is now computed from them, 9.8 × 10¹³ kg, and the doubling count is the exact soonest finish: one more doubling pays while the Foundry is under the target divided by 2 ln 2. The plan still stops at 27 doublings, so no date moves, but a larger Lattice in the Explorer now grows the Foundry as it should, where before it stopped one doubling short.
- **Finding (v3): The receivers' and the Ring's loss heat falls on Venus.** Version 3 said the receivers' loss heat, 4.0 × 10¹⁹ W, and the heat the catcher tracks shed, 5.4 × 10¹⁹ W, radiate to space and never reach Venus. That is 2.7 × 10¹⁹ W at full power, and with it counted the cooling does not finish in time. The published schedule still leaves this heat out, and the check loss-heat-fits measures it. Options under study: one-sided radiators whose backs, facing Venus, are shielded by stacks of polished foil as in vacuum furnaces, or radiators placed far from the planet. Version 4 pumps the tiles with the light itself, and counts the loss heat of the receivers that remain. ([issue #14](https://github.com/Xaxis/phosphoros.space/issues/14))
- **Finding (v3): The Ring cannot carry the receivers the plan hangs on it.** The stator's mass, 3.0 × 10¹² kg, was said to include the Lattice's receivers. The stator's mass now counts the tracks, tether heads and cables, and the check ring-carries-equipment weighs the rest. - **Correction (v3): Cooling waits for the hardware at Venus.** The Foundry's books counted the Foundry, the Veil, the Lattice and the Ring, but not the Exhaust's tiles and canopies (1.6 × 10¹⁴ kg), the chimneys (1.4 × 10¹³ kg), the receivers and their fins, or the Lanterns, and cooling began the day the Foundry reached full scale, before any of them existed. Together they weigh 2.1 × 10¹⁴ kg, more than the Foundry itself. - **Correction (v3): A computed number is no firmer than what it is computed from.** Derived numbers carried a basis typed by hand, and many computed by physics from invented inputs were marked Physics, among them the Earth-like date itself. - **Correction (v3): Every Brick flies its own transfer, out of Ceres's tilted orbit.** Version 3 charged each launch between windows its cheapest transfer, but timed every arrival as if the Brick flew the standard Hohmann transfer, left out the 7.2° tilt between Ceres's orbit and Venus's, and launched every Brick from Ceres's original orbit, though the recoil moves it outward. ### Version 4: Phosphoros without the Ring

Version 3 ended with five open problems: three in the Braking Ring, one in the loss heat of its receivers and one in the Exhaust's beams. Version 4 closes all five by changing the design, never the physics. The Ring is gone, and the Bricks fall straight into the air. The Lattice's light pumps the Exhaust's tiles directly, the tiles shine over their whole sky, and the pumps are charged for that light's entropy. Only the side of Venus the Lattice sees can be pumped. The plan ships a hundredth of the water, and the Lattice and the tiles grow.

- **Correction (v4): The Braking Ring is retired.** Version 3 needed the Ring to catch every Brick, lower the ice on tethers and carry the Lattice's receivers. It could do none of them: lowering needed 150 times the rotor's spare lift, the catch's forward push was 2,000 times that lift with no way to hand it to Venus, and the receivers weighed 12 times what the rotor could hold up. A rotor that could would be heavier and faster by those same factors. Version 4 builds no Ring. The energy it would have recovered, which version 3 counted as supply from the first catch, goes with it, so the budget counts only the Lattice. ([issue #3](https://github.com/Xaxis/phosphoros.space/issues/3))
- **Correction (v4): The Bricks fall straight into the air, so Venus gets seas, not oceans.** Nothing catches the Bricks now. Each one bursts in the air beyond [60°](numbers.md#ice-drop-latitude) of latitude, clear of the canopies, and its whole fall from far out, [147 MJ/kg](numbers.md#ice-entry-energy-per-kg), becomes heat the pumps must lift. Version 3's 10²⁰ kg of ice would have brought 18 times the heat of Venus's whole atmosphere that way, and an ocean in Earth's proportion would bring [350](numbers.md#ocean-entry-vs-atmosphere) times. The plan ships [1.0 × 10¹⁸ kg](numbers.md#ice-mass), enough to cover Venus [2.17 m](numbers.md#ocean-depth) deep, which gathers as seas in the lowlands. Its entry heat is [28%](numbers.md#ice-entry-vs-atmosphere) of the atmosphere's own. The launches take [90 d](numbers.md#ice-campaign-days), from [9 October 2033](numbers.md?date=day#ice-launch-start) instead of running into 2035, so the Bricks fall into air that is still hot. ([issue #2](https://github.com/Xaxis/phosphoros.space/issues/2))
- **Correction (v4): The Lattice's light pumps the tiles directly.** Version 3 turned the Lattice's beams into electricity and sent it to the pumps, but the receivers had nowhere to go: the Ring could not carry them, and on the canopies their fins shone on Venus. The Exhaust's tiles now take the light itself. Each tile is a semiconductor with a bandgap of [0.54 eV](numbers.md#exhaust-bandgap), pumped at [2.5 µm](numbers.md#helios-wavelength), a little below it, so every photon it absorbs leaves as one of its own, a little bluer, with the difference taken from heat. Lasers already cool some crystals this way in the laboratory. The Lattice's efficiency loses its receiver and rises from 15% to [20%](numbers.md#helios-efficiency). Only the Railheads, the Forges and the chimney fans still run on receivers. The check exhaust-pump-photons confirms that each photon has room for the heat the model has it carry. ([issue #15](https://github.com/Xaxis/phosphoros.space/issues/15))
- **Correction (v4): The tiles shine over their whole sky, and the pumps pay for their light's entropy.** Version 3's tiles fired their light into 25° cones, light that counts as 9,000 K by its entropy, and charged the pumps as if it were 1,600 K: lifting heat into it needed 9.6 times the work charged. The tiles now shine evenly over the whole sky above them, which carries the least entropy light that bright can, and every pump in the model, the simulation's included, is charged as if it rejected its heat at the temperature that light counts as, [3,500 K](numbers.md#exhaust-light-temp). The cones' dark strip and their beam-safety distance are gone. Straight above a canopy its light falls below the far-field limit beyond [37,000 km](numbers.md#exhaust-safe-distance), and the lit tiles send the Veil [5.1%](numbers.md#exhaust-veil-load-share) of the sunlight it already turns away. ([issue #4](https://github.com/Xaxis/phosphoros.space/issues/4))
- **Correction (v4): Every receiver's loss heat is lifted with the rest.** Version 3's published schedule left out the receivers' loss heat, 2.7 × 10¹⁹ W of it falling on Venus. With the pumps taking the light itself, the receivers that remain power only the Forges and the fans. They sit on the canopies and shed [2.9 × 10¹⁷ W](numbers.md#venus-receiver-loss) into the air at full output, and the simulation counts it day by day with the heat from the crust, the Forges, the fans, the Bricks and their water. Nothing the plan makes is left out of the heat the pumps lift, so the loss-heat check is retired. ([issue #14](https://github.com/Xaxis/phosphoros.space/issues/14))
- **Correction (v4): Only the side of Venus the Lattice sees can be pumped.** Pumped by light, a tile works only while the Lattice can reach it. The Lattice orbits inside Venus's orbit, so it sees only the sunward side, [50%](numbers.md#exhaust-lit-share) of the canopies at once, and the lit tiles must shine for all of them. At 1,600 K they would need more than the whole lit side. A mirror hovering behind Venus cannot relay the beams to the night side: to hold still its push must point straight away from Venus, and then the beam it sends on misses Venus by as much as the beam it takes in. The tiles now shine as brightly as a [2,200 K](numbers.md#exhaust-effective-temp) black body and peak at [2.2 × 10²⁰ W](numbers.md#exhaust-peak), over [72%](numbers.md#exhaust-area-share) of Venus, [83%](numbers.md#canopy-band-cover) of the band between the drop zones. Their light counts as hotter, so the pumps need more of it, and the Lattice grows to [2.2 × 10²⁰ W](numbers.md#helios-power). Only the lit canopies' chimneys carry heat, so the chimneys widen to [6 km](numbers.md#chimney-diameter).
- **Correction (v4): The Foundry grows to build the hardware at Venus as well.** Version 3 sized the Foundry to finish the Lattice and the Ring soonest, then built the hardware at Venus, heavier than both, at that size. The Foundry now grows to the size that finishes the Lattice and the hardware together soonest: [28](numbers.md#foundry-doublings) doublings, to [2.7 × 10¹⁴ kg](numbers.md#foundry-final-mass). It reaches full scale later, on [31 August 2033](numbers.md?date=day#foundry-full-scale), but builds the [1.9 × 10¹⁴ kg](numbers.md#venus-hardware-mass) of hardware in [61 d](numbers.md#venus-hardware-build-days), so cooling begins sooner, on [23 February 2034](numbers.md?date=day#cooling-start).
- **Correction (v4): The energy budget is counted in light.** The Lattice's output is now the light it beams, [2.2 × 10²⁰ W](numbers.md#helios-power), not the electricity receivers would make from it. What runs on electricity, the Railheads, the Forges and the fans, is charged the light its receivers need; the pumps and the warming take light directly. The plan needs [1.6 × 10²⁸ J](numbers.md#budget-demand) against the [2.2 × 10²⁸ J](numbers.md#helios-supply) the Lattice beams from full power to the deadline: [30%](numbers.md#budget-spare) spare.
- **Correction (v4): The air clears sooner, then the Bricks warm it back.** Cooling starts sooner and the air's sensible heat goes quickly, so the Clear Rain begins on [8 August 2034](numbers.md?date=day#traj-clear-rain). The Bricks arrive after that, from [13 November 2034](numbers.md?date=day#first-arrival) to [23 January 2035](numbers.md?date=day#last-arrival), faster than the lit canopies' chimneys carry heat out, so the air warms back [88 K](numbers.md#traj-rewarm) and the first seas boil back into the air until the Clear Rain begins for good on [30 January 2035](numbers.md?date=day#traj-rerain). Their heat is lifted from air below 304 K, where it costs more to lift than at the start: the seas are frozen through on [30 April 2036](numbers.md?date=day#traj-frozen), and the Frost Towers finish on [26 June 2036](numbers.md?date=day#traj-scavenged), [67 d](numbers.md#frost-sealing-slack) before the last caps. The new water rains out on [26 November 2034](numbers.md?date=day#traj-water-rain), freezes on [30 June 2035](numbers.md?date=day#traj-water-frozen) and melts again once the Veil opens. Venus is Earth-like on [30 September 2036](numbers.md?date=day#traj-earth-like), as before.
- **Correction (v4): The launches are booked on their real dates.** Version 3 averaged the launch schedule over every phase of the Ceres-Venus cycle and over the whole of Ceres's orbit, since its campaign spanned more than two cycles. Version 4's campaign is a third of one cycle, so where the planets are on those days sets the price. The model now places Ceres and Venus by their measured orbits on each launch day and books every day's Bricks onto the cheapest transfer that leaves an arrival slot free. On [9 October 2033](numbers.md?date=day#ice-launch-start) Venus leads Ceres by [214°](numbers.md#ice-launch-lead), [7.9 d](numbers.md#ice-window-wait) before a Hohmann window, so the first shots cost about a Hohmann launch and the last ones more: [19%](numbers.md#launch-offwindow-mean) more on average. Paying launch energy for slower arrivals does not pay here: spread over months, they warm the air back less as they land, but the later ones fall into colder air, and the frost margin is no better.
- **Correction (v4): The Forges wait for the seas they float on.** The Forges are rafts on the liquid CO₂ seas, but version 3 started them on 1 January 2035, three weeks before its Clear Rain began. In version 4 the Bricks' heat boils the first seas back into the air, and they return for good on [30 January 2035](numbers.md?date=day#traj-rerain). The Forges now start on [1 April 2035](numbers.md?date=day#forge-start), and a check holds them to the seas. They have the same oxygen to make in less time, so they run at [5.6 × 10¹⁷ W](numbers.md#forge-power).
